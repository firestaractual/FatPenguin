# Fat Penguin — Art Direction

Last updated: 2026-10-01

## Tone

**Cartoony, but based on real ecology.** The comedy comes from how penguins really behave (shoving, tobogganing, stealing) rather than from made-up gags. Predators are threatening but never gory: a catch is a snap, a splash and a cloud of feathers, then cut away.

The climate theme stays in the background, through the environment: ice breaking off, fish moving farther away. It's never a lecture, and never the emotional arc of the game.

## Logo

**Concept:** an overstuffed penguin falling through cracking ice.

- The penguin is mid-fall: the round, overfed body is stuck halfway through a hole in the ice, flippers up, with a startled expression. A fish tail sticks out of its beak.
- Cracks spread out from the hole across the ice.
- The wordmark "FAT PENGUIN" is chunky and rounded, with some ice texture and maybe a crack running through it.
- **It has to read at app-icon size.** At 48 px, the silhouette (a round penguin plus crack lines) must still say "fat penguin, in trouble." Make an icon-only crop of the penguin and cracks with no wordmark.
- Thin ice is a title-screen gag, not a mechanic (DECISIONS, 2026-10-01). The title screen animates the logo: feed the penguin until it drops through. In play, the joke still holds in a different way: fat penguins are terrible jumpers.

## Readability rules (gameplay comes first)

1. **Body size shows energy.** It's the only energy display, so the difference between thin and fat must be obvious from the gameplay camera, for every penguin on screen. Exaggerate the width; keep the height about the same.
2. **One danger color.** A single reserved color is used for predator lock-ons, lunge lines and wave warnings, and nowhere else (no UI, no cosmetics).
3. **Every attack gets a warning first.** Shadow → swell or bulge → line, always in that order. No attack without one.
4. **Bumps read instantly.** A penguin on its belly (a puck that will fly far) must look clearly different from one on its feet (braced). A hard bump gets a honk, a feather puff and a brief hit-stop; a soft one is just a nudge. Cracks come in a few clearly different widths, so players can judge which ones their current body can hop.
5. **Food pulses are visible from far away.** Seabirds circling and diving over a pulse can be seen from anywhere on the map.
6. **Water depth is readable.** Shallow water is light and deep water is dark. Predators under the surface show up as shadows, not as clear models.

## Characters

- **Penguins:** silhouettes accurate to the species, with exaggerated round bodies. [Open] The hero species. Adélie is the comedy favorite: the white eye ring reads well and gives expressive faces.
- **Leopard seal:** long, smiling, reptile-like head. Creepy-cute, not horrifying.
- **Orcas:** big and graceful. Mostly seen as fins and shadows until they lunge.
- **Seabirds:** mostly background, used as food signals.

## Camera

- **Water:** third-person follow camera, behind and slightly above, pulling back as speed rises.
- **On ice:** pulls up and back so the player can see the waddle and the edges.
- **Kill screen:** a fixed or slow-orbit view that shows the whole floe and every warning.

## Palette (starting point)

| Use | Direction |
|---|---|
| Ocean | Deep blue, getting darker with depth |
| Ice | White and pale cyan (the title screen's thin ice can be gray-blue and see-through) |
| Penguins | Black and white, with warm accents on the beak and feet |
| Danger | One saturated warning color (e.g. hot red-orange), reserved |
| Food | Silver shimmer for fish; pink-orange for krill |
